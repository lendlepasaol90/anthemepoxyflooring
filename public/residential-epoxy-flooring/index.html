Residential epoxy flooring · Anthem, AZ

Seamless interior floors with a designer finish.

Coated concrete turns game rooms, casitas, laundry rooms and flex spaces into durable, easy-care rooms — understated or show-stopping.

When concrete becomes the finished floor

There’s a quiet trend in Anthem homes: more people are choosing coated concrete as an interior floor. Sometimes it starts with practicality — a laundry room that needs to handle a leaking hose, a dog room, a home gym where weights get dropped. Sometimes it’s about style, with metallic or terrazzo-look finishes in game rooms, casitas and open kitchens.

Either way, the appeal is the same. A coated floor is seamless. There’s no grout to stain, no planks to cup if water gets underneath, no carpet holding onto pet hair and dust. Kids, dogs and chair legs barely faze it, and when it finally looks tired a fresh clear coat brings it back.

Inside the house, we work differently than in a garage. We favor low-odor products, contain dust with plastic barriers and vacuum-equipped grinders, and pay close attention to transitions where the coated floor meets tile, carpet or wood.

Where interior coatings make the most sense

  • Game rooms and media rooms — durable under pool cues, bar stools and kids, with a finish that can be as bold as you like
  • Casitas and guest suites — practical floors for detached or attached guest spaces, often converted from storage
  • Laundry and mudrooms — water-tolerant and easy to wipe, with an optional cove edge
  • Home gyms — tough enough for dropped dumbbells, with rubber mats where heavy lifting happens
  • Kitchens and open plans — a seamless look from cabinets to patio doors
  • Pet rooms — easy to sanitize and kind to paws when finished in satin with fine texture

Why homeowners choose it

Nowhere for dirt to hide

One continuous surface means no grout lines, seams or fibers collecting dust and dander.

Water-tolerant

Spills and appliance leaks stay on top instead of seeping under flooring or into subfloors.

Pet-friendly

Accidents clean up fully, and a satin finish with light texture is comfortable for paws.

Cool in summer

Concrete stays cooler underfoot than carpet — welcome in west-facing rooms.

Design range

From quiet satin neutrals to dramatic metallic and terrazzo-style flake.

Renewable

Wear happens in the topcoat, which can be refreshed years later without starting over.

Finish options inside the home

Satin solid color

Warm greige, sand and soft gray tones that pair with most interiors. Satin hides footprints better than gloss.

Micro-flake terrazzo look

Tiny flakes lightly broadcast for a refined speckle reminiscent of terrazzo — great in laundry rooms and mudrooms.

Metallic

Hand-worked pearl pigments for a stone or liquid-metal effect. A natural fit for game rooms, bars and casitas.

Cove edge

The coating curves a few inches up the wall to create a seamless, sealed perimeter that’s easy to clean.

Our interior process

  1. 01

    Plan around your household

    We talk through schedules, pets, furniture and appliances, and how you’ll move around the house during the project.

  2. 02

    Remove existing flooring

    Old flooring is pulled and leftover glue is ground off, leaving bare concrete to bond with.

  3. 03

    Contain the work area

    Doorways are sealed with plastic, vents are covered and dust collection runs throughout.

  4. 04

    Grind, fill and level

    Cracks and low spots are repaired and feathered smooth. Interior finishes show more, so this stage gets extra time.

  5. 05

    Build the finish

    Each layer — sealer, color or design, then clear — is given time to set before the next.

  6. 06

    Return the room

    Air keeps moving while it cures, and we give you the green light for rugs, sofas and the washer.

Moisture, post-tension slabs and other details

Interior slabs in newer Anthem homes are usually in good condition, but they’re still tested. Concrete can transmit moisture vapor from the soil below, and under a dense coating that vapor can cause blistering. A quick test tells us whether a moisture-tolerant primer belongs in your system.

Post-tension foundations are common here too. Interior prep is surface grinding only — we never cut or drill into the slab. Where the coated floor meets other flooring, we use transition details that sit on top of the concrete rather than being cut into it.

Why interior floors call for a careful crew

A garage floor covered in flake forgives small flaws. A satin interior floor in a sunlit room does not. Roller lines, patched cracks that weren’t feathered perfectly and slight dips in the slab all show. The difference between a floor that looks like a design choice and one that looks like a shortcut comes down to patient prep and consistent application.

Inside a lived-in home, there’s also the matter of courtesy: protecting cabinets and baseboards, managing odor, keeping the rest of the house clean and working around family routines. These details make a project pleasant rather than stressful.

In a garage, flake hides small flaws. Indoors, preparation is everything.

Casitas and converted spaces

Many Anthem homes have a casita, a converted storage room or a garage bay turned into a gym or studio. These spaces often have slabs that slope slightly, sit lower than the house, or carry old garage stains. A coating can address all three: low spots can be built up, stains are ground away, and a consistent finish makes the room feel like part of the home.

If you’re converting part of a garage, we can also coat the remaining garage area in a matching or complementary finish, so the two spaces feel intentionally related rather than patched together.

Living with a coated interior floor

Dry mop regularly with microfiber, and damp mop with a neutral cleaner when needed. Avoid oil soaps, waxes and vinegar-heavy mixtures. Felt pads under chairs and furniture prevent scratches, and a mat at exterior doors keeps desert grit from acting like sandpaper. When busy paths eventually dull, a light abrade-and-recoat brings the sheen back.

What shapes the price of an interior floor

Interior projects vary more than garages. Removing tile and thinset takes longer than pulling carpet. Metallic finishes take more labor and material than satin solids. A moisture-tolerant primer, cove edges and extra leveling each add a step. Line items in your quote show where labor goes, so you can choose the extras that matter.

Pets, kids and real life

Households with dogs are among our happiest interior-floor customers. A coated floor doesn’t hold odors, wipes clean after accidents and doesn’t snag claws the way carpet loops can. A satin finish with fine texture gives paws a bit of grip. With little ones, sippy-cup spills, marker and glitter glue sit on top until a wipe takes them away.

If you’re worried about hardness underfoot in play areas or gyms, area rugs and interlocking rubber mats can sit on top of the coating and be moved or replaced as needs change.

Home gyms and workout rooms

Converted garages and spare rooms often become home gyms. A coated floor stands up to dropped dumbbells, sweaty equipment and chalk, and it cleans up quickly. We recommend a satin, lightly textured finish for secure footing, with rubber mats in heavy lifting areas. Lighter colors brighten the room and make it a more pleasant place to train.

Common questions

Is there a strong smell?

We use low-odor products wherever they meet the performance needed, seal off the work area and ventilate. Most families stay home during interior projects.

Can you coat over existing tile?

Tile should come up so the new finish grips the concrete directly. Coating over tile ties the floor’s life to the tile adhesive.

Will a coated floor feel cold?

Concrete feels cooler than carpet, which most people enjoy in Arizona. Area rugs add warmth in seating areas during winter.

Can you match a color to my cabinets?

The color range is broad, and custom tinting can usually get close to a target shade. Bring a sample to the estimate.

How long until we can use the room?

Light foot traffic usually resumes within a day of the topcoat, with furniture returning a day or two later.

Are interior coatings safe with radiant floor heat?

Yes. Coatings sit on top of the slab and prep is surface-only, so embedded systems are not disturbed.

Can you coat just one room?

Absolutely. Many interior projects are a single laundry room, casita or game room.

Will a coated floor affect resale?

Buyers usually see a tough, polished floor in a casita or utility room as a plus. We recommend timeless colors in shared living areas.

Do you move furniture and appliances?

We can help plan it. Large appliances need to be disconnected; we’ll talk through what needs moving during the quote.
