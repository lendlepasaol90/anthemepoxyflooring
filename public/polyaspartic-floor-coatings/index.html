Polyaspartic floor coatings · Anthem, AZ

Finished in a day. Built for Arizona sun.

Polyaspartic cures in hours, stays clear under UV and stands up to hot tires — the reason it tops nearly every garage floor we install.

The chemistry behind a one-day floor

Polyaspartic is a two-component coating from the polyurea family. When its two parts are mixed, a chemical reaction hardens the material in a matter of hours — much faster than the days-long cure of conventional epoxy. It was first developed for corrosion protection on steel structures, where durability and quick return to service are critical. Those same traits make it ideal for garage floors.

Speed is only half the story. Polyaspartic is aliphatic, which means it doesn’t yellow when exposed to sunlight. Standard epoxy is aromatic and will gradually amber under UV, often showing a visible band just inside the garage door. In a place with as much sunshine as Anthem, that difference matters.

Ways we use polyaspartic

Most commonly, polyaspartic is the final, protective layer over an epoxy or polyurea base. The base handles bonding and build; the polyaspartic handles sun, abrasion and chemicals. On projects where time is the priority, we build the whole system — base, flake and top — from fast-curing polyurea and polyaspartic products, which is what allows a one-day garage.

It also revives tired, amber-tinted epoxy and serves as a grippy clear layer for sun-baked porches and patio covers.

Why it isn’t a DIY coating

The same reactivity that makes polyaspartic fast makes it challenging to apply. Once mixed, the working time can be just minutes — shorter on a warm slab. Roll too slowly and lap marks appear; mix too much and it gels in the bucket. Retail versions are slowed down so far to be manageable that they lose much of what makes polyaspartic worthwhile.

We control batch sizes, measure slab temperature and select the product speed for the season. That control is what produces a smooth, uniform finish on a one-day timeline.

Fast cure is a feature for the homeowner and a challenge for the installer.

Polyaspartic and the Anthem climate

Anthem’s garages often face west or south, and doors stay open while people unload groceries, work on projects or chat with neighbors. Strong afternoon sunlight reaches deep into the floor. A polyaspartic topcoat is the simplest way to keep that floor from yellowing.

Summer heat speeds up curing, so we work early and use slower-reacting grades. In the cooler months, faster grades keep projects on schedule. During monsoon season, the dense polyaspartic surface sheds water and dust and rinses clean with a hose.

Refreshing a yellowed epoxy floor

An older epoxy garage that has gone honey-colored can often be rescued rather than stripped. If the existing coating is still firmly attached, we clean it, scuff-sand it so the new layer can grip, repair any damage and apply a new polyaspartic topcoat. A light broadcast of fresh flake can help blend old discoloration. If the coating is peeling, removal is the better path, and we’ll be upfront about which applies.

Is a one-day floor right for you?

For most families, yes. When the garage is command central for cars, bikes and soccer bags, a week without it is a headache. A one-day system minimizes that.

A slower build still wins sometimes — damp slabs needing a vapor-blocking epoxy primer, sprawling or complicated layouts, or budgets better served by an epoxy base under a polyaspartic top. We’ll lay out the trade-offs honestly.

Preparing for installation day

Empty the slab the evening prior, even the boxes hiding beneath shelving. Park vehicles on the driveway or street. If you have a garage refrigerator, freezer or gun safe, let us know so we can plan around it. Keep pets and kids out of the garage during the work and until we give the all-clear.

You’re welcome to stay home. We seal the house door, keep air moving and tape a reminder on the door so no one steps in early.

Where polyaspartic is especially valuable

Beyond garages, polyaspartic shines anywhere sunlight and traffic meet.

  • West- and south-facing garages that stay open in the afternoon
  • Front entries and covered porches in full sun
  • Covered patios and outdoor kitchens
  • Sunrooms and Arizona rooms with large windows
  • Recoats of older, yellowed epoxy floors

Caring for a polyaspartic floor

Run a dust mop often, particularly after a breezy day, since sand is the real enemy of shine. Mop with warm water and a neutral cleaner when needed. Wipe up pool chemicals, fertilizer or battery fluid promptly. A citrus degreaser removes most tire marks. Rubber mats under heavy trailer tires help spread the load.

What affects the cost of a polyaspartic floor

Polyaspartic materials cost more per gallon than standard epoxy, and one-day systems require a larger, coordinated crew. Preparation is the other major factor: removing old paint or failed coatings, repairing cracks and addressing moisture can add time. Flake coverage, finish choice and garage size round out the picture. Your quote lists each item so you can compare a one-day polyaspartic system with a traditional multi-day option side by side.

Polyaspartic outdoors

Because it resists UV, polyaspartic is one of the best topcoats for exterior concrete. Covered patios, front porches and courtyard entries all benefit from its color stability. Outdoors, we always add texture to the topcoat for wet conditions and choose lighter colors that stay comfortable in the sun. It also stands up well to pool chemicals, making it a strong choice for spa surrounds and pool-adjacent patios.

Pairing polyaspartic with flake

Most of our one-day garages combine a fast-cure base, a full flake broadcast and a polyaspartic clear coat. The flake adds texture and hides dust; the polyaspartic locks it in and keeps the colors from fading. It’s a combination that balances looks, durability and speed better than any single product on its own.

A quick comparison: one-day vs. multi-day

Think of it as a trade between calendar time and cost. A same-day system costs somewhat more and asks more of the crew, but your garage is out of action for roughly a day. A multi-day epoxy build costs a little less in materials and suits damp or complex slabs, but the space is tied up for most of a week. Both are durable when the prep is right — the choice comes down to how much that week of downtime is worth to your household.

Common questions

Is polyaspartic better than epoxy?

They work best together. Epoxy bonds and builds thickness well; polyaspartic resists sun and wear and cures fast. Most of our systems use both.

Is a same-day floor less durable?

Yes, when properly installed. Longevity depends on preparation and thickness, not cure speed.

When can I park?

Usually within a few days, depending on temperature. We confirm exact timing before we leave.

Can it be installed in July?

Yes. We start early and use slower-reacting grades designed for warm slabs.

Is there an odor?

Some odor during application that fades quickly with ventilation. We keep the door to the house closed.

Can polyaspartic go over my existing epoxy floor?

Often, if the existing coating is firmly bonded. We clean, sand, repair and recoat. Peeling coatings must be removed first.

Will polyaspartic yellow over time?

No. It’s aliphatic, so it resists the UV-driven yellowing that affects standard epoxy.

Does polyaspartic work in a golf cart or toy bay?

Yes. It resists abrasion from turning tires and the chemicals that can drip from batteries and engines.
