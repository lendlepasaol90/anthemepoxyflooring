Patio & concrete coatings · Anthem, AZ

Outdoor floors for outdoor living.

Sun-stable, textured coatings that refresh patios, pool decks and courtyards — cooler underfoot, secure when wet and easy to keep clean.

Backyards have become living rooms

Anthem backyards have changed a lot since the community’s early days. What began as a simple slab under a patio cover has, in many homes, grown into a full outdoor living space: built-in grills and kitchens, fire pits, shade sails, misting systems and seating for a crowd. Pools and spas sit a few steps away, and the view of Daisy Mountain or the surrounding hills is part of the design.

Often the one piece left behind is the concrete itself. Years of sun, sprinkler overspray, leaf stains, grill grease and minor settling leave patios looking tired and patchy. A coating gives that surface a fresh, unified finish — and seals it so future spills and stains wash away rather than soak in.

What an outdoor coating improves

Safer when wet

Texture built into the topcoat helps bare and wet feet grip near pools, spas and steps.

Cooler colors

Light, reflective finishes absorb less heat than dark concrete or paint.

Stain resistance

Grease, wine, sunscreen and leaf tannins rinse off a sealed surface.

Uniform finish

Covers patchwork repairs, old discoloration and mismatched pours.

UV stability

Exterior-grade clear coats hold color where indoor epoxy would chalk and amber.

Outdoor finish options

Textured flake

A decorative chip blend sealed with a textured topcoat. Light, warm blends stay cooler and hide dust.

Solid color with grit

A single color coordinated to stucco and trim, with aggregate for traction.

Stone-look blends

Multi-tone chips that resemble granite or flagstone — at home beside desert landscaping.

Clear protective sealer

Love the pattern you already have? A transparent sealer shields stamped or tinted concrete and leaves the design visible.

How outdoor projects are installed

  1. 01

    Evaluate drainage and joints

    We look at slope, low spots, cracks and expansion joints, and how water leaves the slab.

  2. 02

    Deep clean and grind

    Organic stains, efflorescence and old sealers are removed and the surface is opened.

  3. 03

    Detail joints and cracks

    Cracks are filled; expansion joints are handled so the slab can move without cracking the coating.

  4. 04

    Color layer

    The base coat and decorative chips or pigment are applied.

  5. 05

    Textured topcoat

    A UV-stable topcoat with traction grit seals the surface.

  6. 06

    Cure and return

    We advise on foot traffic, furniture and grill placement timing.

Why so many patio coatings fail

The most common patio failures we see trace back to a few mistakes. Interior epoxy was used outdoors and turned chalky and yellow. The surface was left smooth and became slick by the pool. A coating was applied over a builder sealer it couldn’t bond to. Or the coating was rolled continuously across expansion joints and cracked along every one within the first year.

Our answer is simple: exterior-grade chemistry, sealers ground off, grip mixed in, and joints left free to flex.

Outdoor coatings fail for predictable reasons — and each one is preventable.

Pool decks and spas

Around water, the finish has to grip wet feet without scraping skin. A light, sun-proof top layer gets a sprinkle of grit, and we try it barefoot on a test patch first. Our outdoor systems tolerate chlorine and salt, and sealing the deck keeps salt from crystallizing inside the concrete over time.

Coping, drains and the transition to pavers or turf are detailed carefully so water flows off the deck rather than pooling at the edges.

Front entries, courtyards and HOA considerations

Front courtyards and entry walks set the tone for a home. A coating can give them a crisp, consistent finish with secure footing. Because these areas are visible from the street, many Anthem neighborhoods have guidelines about exterior colors and materials. We recommend checking with your HOA before choosing a color for visible surfaces, and we’re glad to provide physical samples and product information for any approval request.

Covered back patios and pool decks are usually less visible and offer more freedom, but it never hurts to confirm.

Choosing an outdoor color

In direct sun, dark surfaces absorb far more heat than light ones and can become uncomfortable by mid-afternoon. For uncovered patios and pool decks we recommend lighter tones: sand, cream, soft gray and pale stone blends. These also complement Anthem’s typical stucco and roof palettes.

Mid-tone, multi-color blends hide wind-blown dust best. Very light or very dark finishes show it quickly. If you’re unsure, we’ll bring samples you can lay out in the actual sun exposure of your patio.

Keeping your patio looking great

Blow or sweep off debris and rinse with a hose as needed. Use a mild degreaser and soft brush for grill spills. If you pressure wash, use a wide fan tip and keep a reasonable distance. Put glides on metal furniture feet and lift planters off the surface so water doesn’t sit underneath. Adjust sprinkler heads to keep hard water off the slab.

Planning around the seasons

Outdoor coatings go down best in moderate, dry weather. Fall, winter and spring are ideal in Anthem — conveniently, the same seasons when people most want to use their patios. Summer projects begin at sunrise, and if storm cells are brewing we’d rather push the date than gamble on a fresh coat.

If you’re planning a party, a holiday or visiting family, tell us when we schedule. We’ll build in enough cure time for the patio to be ready.

What affects the price of a patio coating

Size is only part of the picture. Patios with heavy staining, efflorescence or old sealers need more preparation. Drainage corrections, crack repairs and joint detailing add time. Pool decks require extra attention around coping and drains. Flake and stone-look finishes cost more than a solid color or clear sealer. Your quote itemizes these so you can choose where to invest.

Courtyard entries and side yards

Many Anthem homes have a gated front courtyard — a small outdoor room between the street and the front door. These spaces often have a mix of concrete, pavers and planting beds, and the concrete tends to stain from irrigation and leaf litter. A coating with a stone-look blend can make a courtyard feel finished and welcoming while giving guests secure footing.

Side yards and RV pads are practical candidates as well. A sealed, textured surface along the side of the house is easier to keep clean and stays free of oil stains where a trailer or boat is parked.

Outdoor kitchens and fire features

Outdoor kitchens bring cooking grease, sauces and spilled drinks onto the patio. A sealed coating keeps them from staining, and a mid-tone color hides the inevitable drips between cleanings. Around fire pits and fireplaces, we keep coatings at a safe distance from direct flame and recommend stone or tile hearths where embers might land. Planning these details with you during the quote keeps the finished space both beautiful and practical.

Coating around pavers, turf and planters

Many patios meet pavers, artificial turf, gravel or planting beds. These edges need care: the coating should stop cleanly at a defined line, be sealed so water can’t creep underneath, and leave room for the neighboring material to drain. We tape and detail these transitions by hand, and we recommend lifting planters onto feet so water doesn’t sit against the new surface.

Common questions

Will it be too hot in summer?

Lighter colors stay much cooler than dark ones. We match the color to your patio’s sun exposure.

Is the finish safe by the pool?

Yes — we build in fine aggregate so it grips when wet, and our outdoor systems resist pool chemicals.

Can you coat stamped concrete?

We can seal it clear to preserve the pattern or grind and recoat it for an entirely new look.

How soon can we use the patio?

Expect to walk on it the next day and set the lounge chairs back a day or so later.

Do I need HOA approval?

For visible areas like front entries, possibly. Back patios are usually less restricted. We can provide samples for your submission.

Does the coating resist sprinkler stains?

Yes. Hard-water spots rinse off a sealed surface much more easily than bare concrete. Adjusting sprinkler heads also helps.

Can a coating fix a patio that holds water?

Small dips can occasionally be filled during preparation. Larger drainage problems are better solved with concrete work first, and we’ll tell you if that’s the case.

Can you coat my front walkway too?

Yes. Walkways and courtyard entries are popular additions; just check HOA guidelines for visible areas first.
